# Homebrew nature — Cats

A proposal for a sixth playable nature in *Fallen London: the Roleplaying Game*,
built to sit beside the corebook's five without outclassing them. Not official.

## What the book already gives us

Cats appear in chapter 1 (p. 34) as setting colour, closed with a "Cats in Play"
box that frames them as **NPCs**. Everything below is drawn from that entry and
from the Parabola section (p. 52–53); nothing is invented where canon speaks.

- They talk — "formally, pointedly, and about a dizzying range of topics" — but
  only when they choose. Some Londoners have no idea cats can speak; the cats
  do not consider them worth the conversation.
- They prize secrets, and are equipped to gather them: agility, quiet padded
  feet, charm.
- **In a mirror, a cat's reflection is a great cat** — lioness, panther, jaguar,
  leopard. In Parabola they wear that shape and go to war. Never tigers; tigers
  stand apart.
- Their ancient purpose is to contest the territories of dream against the
  serpents and hold the Fingerkings back. They know Parabola's unpredictable
  geography and the wiles and weaknesses of snakes.
- **"They know the trick of stepping through mirrors, which might explain why
  it's impossible to keep them out of anywhere. They keep the secret closely."**
  And, from the access rules for Parabola: passing through a mirror "requires
  Glasswork **or the aid of a cat**."
- Every cat holds a noble title and a Parabolan fief, and interactions between
  cats are contests of precedence. There are no working-class cats; each one
  considers itself superior to any human below the rank of aristocracy.
- Earn a cat's respect — by courtesy, by catching it unawares, by doing it a
  favour — and custom dictates that it tells you a secret.
- In London itself, "apart from the powers of speech, cats are more or less
  mundane." Their power is behind the glass, not on the pavement.
- Origin, if you can befriend a scholar-cat: speech was granted by a god called
  **Stone**, after the opening of the mirrors, with the charge to stand upon the
  borders of dream and protect all that Is from all that Is-Not.

## The design budget

Every corebook nature is built to the same shape, which is what makes a sixth
one possible without guesswork.

| Nature | Wounds | Nightmares | Scandal | Suspicion | Boxes | Threshold economy |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Traditional Human | 3 / 8 | 3 / 8 | 3 / 8 | 3 / 8 | 12 | flat |
| Clay Man | 4 / 9 | 3 / 8 | 3 / 8 | 2 / 8 | 12 | +1 Wounds |
| Rattus faber | 2 / 8 | 3 / 8 | 4 / 9 | 3 / 8 | 12 | +1 Scandal |
| Rubbery Man | 3 / 8 | 4 / 8 | 2 / 6 | 3 / 6 | 12 | −2 Scandal, −2 Suspicion |
| Tomb-Colonist | 3 / 10 | 2 / 8 | 3 / 8 | 4 / 8 | 12 | +2 Wounds, with final death attached |

Three rules fall out of that table, and the Cat keeps all three:

1. **Twelve boxes, always.** The spread differs; the total never does.
2. **Thresholds are the currency of prejudice.** A nature buys a raised
   threshold with a lowered one somewhere, or with a trait that bites.
3. **Everything else is traits.** Three to four per nature.

Two further precedents matter here:

- **A special skill from a nature**: only the Rubbery Man has one — Shapeling
  Arts at Adept, granted by *being* one. The Shapeling Arts *twist* still
  requires the Shaper profession; the nature grant is separate and cheaper.
  This is the exact hook for giving cats Glasswork.
- **An attribute adjustment from a nature**: only Rattus faber has one — "+1 to
  a single attribute, up to a maximum of 2", paid for by Rodenty Stature cutting
  both ways.

## The Cat (settled)

> You are a nobody's pet and a somebody's baroness. On this side of the glass you
> are twelve pounds of opinion with the run of every roof in Spite; on the other
> you are a panther holding a border older than the city, and the serpents know
> your name. You could tell them how you get into locked rooms. You will not.

**Menaces — 12 boxes, two caps lowered, in the Rubbery Man's pattern**

| Menace | Track | Overcome | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wounds | 2 boxes | **6** | Twelve pounds of bone and opinion. A cat that fails to dodge does not get a second opinion about it. |
| Nightmares | 3 boxes | 8 | Half your life is spent behind the glass. Dream does not frighten you the way it frightens Londoners. |
| Scandal | 3 boxes | **6** | Your standing is judged in the feline courts, where precedence is everything and nothing is ever forgiven. |
| Suspicion | 4 boxes | 8 | Nobody suspects a cat. The constabulary does not take statements from cats, and the Department of Menace Eradication has no writ for you. |

Twelve boxes, as every nature has, and two lowered caps — the same shape of
bargain the Rubbery Man makes, moved to different menaces. Note what that means
in play: **no corebook nature lowers its Wounds cap**, so a cat is the frailest
character in the game by a clear margin — the shortest track on the menace that
gets marked most often, ending two levels early.

**Abilities & traits**

- **Every Cat a Lion** *(trait)* — You have the **Glasswork** special skill at
  Adept. In Parabola you wear your true shape: a lion, a panther, a leopard.
  Never a tiger. You may not take the Silverer profession or the Glasswork
  twist: the trick is innate, and Glasswork is a discipline invented by people
  who lack it.
- **The Trick of the Glass** *(ability)* — You may pass through any mirror large
  enough for you, with no preparation, no ritual and no equipment: mark 1 box of
  Nightmares. To carry a companion through, mark 2 boxes instead — and something
  on the other side always notices that a door was used twice.
- **Four Paws** *(trait)* — You go where nothing with hands can follow. Anything
  that turns on going unnoticed, fitting through a gap, climbing, or arriving
  somewhere nobody thought was reachable takes favourable circumstances. What
  paws cannot do is human work: anything needing hands, human reach or human
  strength is a formidable challenge, and you carry one notable item at a time.
- **Small and Soft, and Out of Favour** *(trait)* — Your maximum limits of both
  Wounds and Scandal are reduced; you are overcome by either at level 6 instead
  of 8. Wounds because you are a cat in a city built for creatures ten times your
  size; Scandal because what ruins you is not disgrace among humans, whose
  opinion is beneath you, but a feline court stripping your title and your fief.
- **Nine Lives** *(trait)* — You have nine, and you will not get more. When you
  are overcome by Wounds you die — and then you come back. Strike a life; you
  return at the start of the next intermission with your Wounds track cleared and
  your level reset, without the recovery roll and without costing your concern
  the time it costs everyone else. But you do not take the Boatman's ferry. You
  wake in Parabola wearing your true shape, and you have to find a mirror home,
  and each time you come back a little more the panther and a little less the
  cat: **raise your Nightmares level by 1**, and the GM says what your reflection
  kept that you did not — a memory, a face, a name, a fief.
  With no lives left, the next death is final. A cat down to its last life is a
  careful cat, and every other cat can tell at a glance.

- **Precedence and Custom** *(trait)* — You hold a title and a fief in Parabola.
  Assert it and take favourable circumstances with any cat of lesser standing —
  but mark 1 Scandal if a cat of higher standing is present to hear it. And when
  anyone shows you courtesy, catches you unawares, or does you a favour, custom
  obliges you to give them a secret; refuse, and mark 1 Scandal.
- **Old Scars** *(optional trait)* — Cats are old, and a PC has probably been
  living dangerously already. You may start the game with up to three of your
  nine lives already spent; take **2 further Area of Expertise levels** for each
  (enough for a new area at Veteran), tied to the war behind the glass. The
  scars are old, so they do not raise your Nightmares level. The lives stay
  struck for the whole game and are never refunded in play.

Six entries, plus one optional — more than any corebook nature, because two of
them are the halves of a single bargain: the caps that make a cat die easily,
and the lives that make it come back. No attribute adjustment: the Rattus faber keeps that
distinction, and the Cat's compensation is Glasswork.

**Common presumptions**

- Cats talk, but only when it suits them. Plenty of Londoners have never heard one.
- Every cat is nobility, with a title and an estate nobody can point to on a map.
- Do a cat a courtesy and it owes you a secret. This is known, and exploited.
- They get in everywhere. Nobody knows how, and the cats prefer it that way.

**Secrets**

- A god called Stone granted cats their speech after the opening of the mirrors,
  charging them to guard all that Is from all that Is-Not.
- The trick of stepping through glass is the secret they guard most tightly. It
  is why no room is ever truly closed.
- Tigers are both ally and rival — the Wakeful Court competes for the same glory
  — and a tiger is a tiger on either side of the glass. No one will say why.
- The war behind the glass is real and ancient, and the day the cats lose it, the
  Fingerkings come out through every mirror in London.

**Play a cat if…**

- you want the city's secrets brought to you by custom rather than prised out by skill;
- you want Parabola as home ground rather than a hazardous expedition;
- you want to be beneath the notice of everyone above you and outranked by everyone with fur;
- you want a character who cannot open a door, and must talk someone into it.

## Balance check

The Cat's grant is the strongest a nature gives — a special skill at Adept, plus
a mirror-trick no other character can buy at any price. Four things pay for it:

- **Two Wounds boxes and a cap of 6** — the frailest body in the game, and the
  only nature whose Wounds cap is lowered at all.
- **Four Paws**, whose second half is the harshest intrinsic limit of any nature:
  the Rubbery Man is merely hard to understand; the cat cannot force a window or
  carry the loot.
- **A Scandal cap of 6**, the Rubbery Man's own penalty, on a track with two named
  ways to mark it that no other nature carries.
- **No attribute adjustment**, so the Cat's 2/1/1/0 is the flattest spread of any
  non-human nature.

Against that, the one thing cats keep for themselves: the longest Suspicion track
in the game. Nobody suspects a cat, and that is the whole joy of playing one.

**Barred combination.** A cat may not take the Silverer profession or the
Glasswork twist. Mechanically this stops a cat Silverer opening at Master
Glasswork; in the fiction, no cat would submit to a human discipline built to
imitate what it was born knowing.

## Decisions taken

| Fork | Chosen |
|---|---|
| Where the price sits | Both — Four Paws *and* lowered caps, with no attribute bump |
| Menace spread | Wounds 2/6 · Nightmares 3/8 · Scandal 3/6 · Suspicion 4/8 |
| Death | Nine Lives — return next intermission at the cost of a life and 1 Nightmares level; the ninth death is final |
| Old Scars | Optional at creation: up to 3 lives pre-spent, 2 AoE levels each; no Nightmares cost |
| Glasswork grant | Adept, matching the Rubbery Man's Shapeling Arts |
| Cat + Silverer | Barred outright |

## If this goes into the ledger

- New entry in the natures list, tagged as homebrew so it reads as distinct from
  the corebook five.
- The Silverer bar becomes a validation rule alongside the existing forced-twist
  checks: picking Cat with Silverer (or the Glasswork twist) raises an error in
  the checks panel and inline on the step.
- Glasswork appears in the sheet's special-skills block with its usual reference
  card — attribute rule, cost, stakes — exactly as it does for a Silverer.

## Why nine lives, and what they cost

The book leaves a real gap here. Humans mostly return from death, by nothing more
than the Neath's lax natural laws. Rattus faber mostly do not — unless St Eligius
pays a visit, at a price. Tomb-colonists roll for it, and eventually lose. Cats:
the book does not say. Folklore does, and folklore fits the canon we do have —
speech was granted by a god called **Stone**, along with the charge to hold the
border of dream. A god who conscripts an army issues it equipment, and nine lives
is the equipment.

What makes the trait earn its place is not the safety net; it is the shape of the
cost.

- **It moves a shared cost onto one character.** Everyone else's recovery expands
  the concern's next intermission — the whole table pays for one PC's Wounds. A
  cat's return costs the table nothing and the cat something irreplaceable. That
  is a different decision at the table, and a better one.
- **It is a countdown, not a cushion.** Nine is generous early and terrifying
  late. A campaign that runs long will see the number get small, and the game
  gets sharper as it does.
- **It pulls in one direction.** Each death raises Nightmares and takes something
  your reflection keeps. A cat that dies often is drifting toward the thing in
  the mirror, which is exactly where this nature's fiction wants to go.
- **It stays inside the frailty.** Wounds 2/6 still means a cat goes down faster
  than anyone. Nine Lives changes what going down *means*, not how often it
  happens.

**Old Scars, if you want the lives to matter at creation.** Cats are old, and a
PC has probably been living dangerously already. Let a player start with fewer
than nine, and take something for each life already spent: two further levels of
Area of Expertise per life, tied to the war behind the glass, to a maximum of
three lives. Two levels buys a new area at Veteran, or two areas at Casual — the
budget is what is enforced, not the shape. A cat with six lives and six extra
levels is a veteran of the border in every sense, and closer to the end than
they would like. The lives spent this way carry no Nightmares cost: the price
was paid long ago, and it is paid in the lives themselves.
