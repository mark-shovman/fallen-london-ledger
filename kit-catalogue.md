# Fallen London — Kit Catalogue

Every notable item from Chapter 6, *Tools of Many Trades*, as the character
ledger uses it. Edit freely: fix wording, move an item to another category,
correct a use count, delete an entry, or add one of your own.

**Format rules — keep these and the file will load back cleanly.**

* `## Name` starts an item. Everything until the next `## ` belongs to it.
* Field lines look like `- Category: Adornments`. Keep the label and the colon.
* `Kind:` is `equipment` (reusable) or `resource` (spent as it is used).
* `Uses:` is a number for resources, or `—` for equipment.
* `- Purpose:` and `- Traits:` are followed by their own bullets, one per line.
  Any `-` line that is not a known field label is a bullet of the list above it.
* `Gain:` and `Lose:` are optional; the Prominences use them.
* Lines outside an item (like this preamble) are ignored.

# Adornments

## Irrigo Locket

- Category: Adornments
- Kind: equipment
- Uses: —
- Tagline: Sometimes you even forget that it sits about your neck. Be careful not to look down.
- Purpose:
  - contain some useful tool hidden within
  - hide a small object to avoid its detection
- Traits:
  - Once per venture, when you need a useful tool, mark 2 boxes of Nightmares to recall the locket and open it up. Any reasonable one-use resource capable of fitting in the locket might be inside. After you use the locket in this way, you must mark 2 boxes of Nightmares to refill it during an intermission and place something else inside, for use in a later venture.
  - Any object small enough to be placed in the locket is nigh-undetectable by others. Mark 2 boxes of Nightmares at the end of a venture to remember it and take it out.

## Starveling Stole

- Category: Adornments
- Kind: equipment
- Uses: —
- Tagline: An unnaturally long, ragged, violent, and spiteful cat that tolerates being worn as a stole in return for regular meals. Occasionally given as a gift as a sign of deep regard, obsessive rivalry, or both. Love is complicated, isn’t it?
- Purpose:
  - gain a companion that doubles as couture
  - intimidate or agitate nearby Rattus faber
  - flatter or burden a target you choose to bestow it upon
- Traits:
  - Once per venture, you may choose someone to receive your Starveling Stole for a time. If they are grateful for your gesture, they will give you a favour of their choice. If they are fond of you, they become a new connection if they weren’t already one, or they become a new mundanity, ‘Confidante’, if they were already a connection. If the target bears ill will toward you, is in a monogamous relationship, or would otherwise face scrutiny for receiving the delivery, they gain 1 level of Scandal, and at the GM’s discretion, they may sever any relationship with you, be it as a connection or mundanity. The Stole always returns to you during your next intermission, insisting upon being tended.
  - The stole counts as a mundanity; you must tend to it during intermissions or risk its wrath.
  - You cannot take favourable circumstances in positive, calm, or pleasant interactions with Rattus faber while wearing the Stole.

## The Neathy Tarot

- Category: Tools
- Kind: equipment
- Uses: —
- Tagline: Portable portents to be concealed in your glove for easy consultation. Good for omens and signals from the beyond. Prone to melodramatic pronouncements of tangled and improbable romance.
- Purpose:
  - predict the nature of a future event
  - decode messages that filter through the Veil
  - fascinate occultists and their sympathizers
- Traits:
  - Once per venture, you may draw a card from your deck. Roll 1d6. On a 1, the card is unlucky; on 2’s and 3’s the card is ominous; on 4’s and 5’s, the card is portentous; on 6’s, the card is lucky. The GM will reveal information based on the nature of the card:
  - For an unlucky card, the GM will reveal a terrible consequence that looms over you. If you miss a challenge roll, you may claim that terrible consequence and have it come to pass (if the GM agrees that it makes sense in the situation); when you do, you may lower your level of Nightmares by 2 in relief, or you may claim an obsession, ‘Predicting the Worst’, at strength 1 (if you are able, reducing other obsessions as needed) or raise a similarly worded obsession by 1.
  - For an ominous card, the GM will reveal a premonition of an upcoming challenge through sense, emotion, colour, and feeling. When that forewarned challenge comes to pass, you may use this equipment to gain a bonus die, no matter the situation.
  - For a portentous card, the GM will reveal a premonition of an ally, asset, or other resource that could help you achieve your current goals; you may ask the GM one question about what you see, and they will answer honestly.
  - For a lucky card, you see a windfall of victories showered upon you. Choose one attribute; once during the rest of this venture, you can add 1 hit to a challenge roll made with that attribute, but only if you already have at least 1 hit on that roll.
